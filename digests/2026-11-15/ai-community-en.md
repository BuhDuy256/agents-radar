## Workflows
### A sender line cut an agent's false "we are talking" claim
The author tested whether a scheduled self-audit message should name who triggered it. On a frozen copy of the audit context, they added one line reading "sender=scheduler, not Keniel; Keniel is not participating in this turn," then scheduled 24 calls per version to `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` at temperature 0.7 and had a model grade 47 replies blind. Claims of an active conversation dropped from 20 of 23 to 2 of 24 (Fisher exact p ≈ 0.0000000365). The result covers one frozen context and one model; the labeled version still made the claim twice. The author suggests testing the same pattern on any agent with scheduled heartbeats or cron audits.

### Quantize the KV cache to fit a 19.5GB model on 24GB VRAM
The author explains why a 32B Q4_K_M model at roughly 19.5 GB of weights plus an FP16 KV cache can exceed a 24 GB GPU, and lays out fixes. Steps: enable Flash Attention, set the KV cache type to `q8_0` to roughly halve cache memory, set parallel sequences to 1, and set an explicit context length such as 16384 through a Modelfile or `llama.cpp` flags like `-ctk q8_0` and `-ctv q8_0`. The author estimates a drop from 32K to 16K context moves KV memory from about 4.29 GB to 2.15 GB. Settings can be overridden by the client; verify with `ollama ps` and `nvidia-smi`.

## Experiments and comparisons
### Better targeting calls did not produce better mission outcomes
The author built a judgment model called Jev to command a bot gunner in a zombie game, then compared three arms on identical seeds across 48 paired missions: no commander, hand-written rules, and Jev. Jev picked a target blocked by buildings 3 of 715 times versus 154 of 706 for the rules, yet win rate, aircraft lost, and survivors saved were statistically indistinguishable, while score per mission fell about 27% (mean −10,460, 95% CI [−13,585, −7,276]). The author attributes the gap to the bot already re-deciding every half second while the commander acted every 15 seconds. They also note an earlier 8-mission-per-arm result about aircraft losses did not hold at larger sample size.

### Swizzle Runtime reports 209 tok/s and 43ms TTFT on a 4GB laptop GPU
The company open-sourced Swizzle Runtime v0.1.0 under Apache-2.0, a Rust binary with AVX2, CUDA, and Vulkan kernels, and published benchmarks against `llama.cpp`. On an AMD Ryzen 7 170 with an RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (4 GB, sm_86, CUDA 12.8), it reports 209.70 tok/s versus 196.84 on `tg128` for Qwen3-0.6B-Q8_0, 43.37 ms TTFT, and larger CPU-side gains (90.61 versus 65.80 on `edit256`). Runs are described as bit-identical greedy parity with llama.cpp, five repetitions with 5s cooldown. These are the company's own numbers on one machine and one pair of small models; they do not show behavior on larger models.
