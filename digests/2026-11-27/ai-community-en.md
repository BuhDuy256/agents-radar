## Experiments and comparisons
### Durable action keys cut duplicate orders to one across eight fault cases
The author tried an HTTP order service with a scripted tool caller, injecting timeouts, dropped connections and process restarts, then compared three caller strategies by counting orders created. Sending no key, or a new UUID per attempt, produced two orders when the caller timed out after commit, and eight when eight deliveries ran concurrently. One UUID saved for the intended action before the first request and reused produced one order in every case. It failed when the service had no record of the earlier request. This is a local reproduction, not production frequency data.
