### Claude Code team says verification loops are what let you run many agents at once
The Claude Code team says roughly 90 percent of its code is written and reviewed by Claude, and that giving agents a way to verify their own output is what makes running dozens of agents in parallel possible instead of one or two. Adding a separate grader that checks Claude's work against a rubric improved task success by up to 10 points in internal tests on structured file generation, the team says. The article recommends end-to-end tests as the first investment, because they are hard to fool and can show what broke, then property tests that generate edge-case inputs, then differential tests that compare an old system against a new one on the same inputs. The team says a failing check that Claude cannot fix in three rounds should stop and bring in a human.

### Harvard working paper: AI coding agents shifted the bottleneck to code review
A working paper by Harvard Ph.D. candidates Fiona Chen and James Stratton, covering 718 firms and 725,938 workers from January 2021 to March 2026, found that engineers at firms adopting AI coding agents added about 30 percent more code, while finished work measured as resolved Jira issues did not measurably rise. Time from pull request submission to merge rose 3.45 days on a 7.03-day baseline, a 49 percent increase, and the share of pull requests receiving a formal change request rose about 12 percentage points from a 13 percent baseline. The paper is a job-market paper with no indication of completed peer review, and the authors note larger firms adopted earlier, which they control for.

### Randomized experiment finds Google AI Overviews cut clicks to outside sites by about 40%
Researchers Saharsh Agarwal and Ananya Sen gave US desktop Chrome users an extension that randomly hid AI Overviews for some of them, then watched two weeks of real searches in January and February 2026. On searches that would show an Overview, the group that saw them averaged 0.37 clicks to outside websites per search against 0.62 for the group that did not, which the authors report as a 39.8 percent reduction, and searches ending with no outbound click rose from 54 percent to 73 percent. Across all searches the authors put the loss at roughly 18.5 percent of organic clicks from Google. The paper is a working paper, not yet peer reviewed, and covers desktop only.
