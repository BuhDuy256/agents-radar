### Harvard Working Paper Says AI Coding Agents Cut Finished Work, Not Review Time

A Harvard working paper by Ph.D. candidates Fiona Chen and James Stratton found that at 718 firms, engineers added about 30% more code after adopting AI coding agents, while resolved Jira issues rose 0.12 per worker-month on a 3.67 baseline — a result the authors call statistically indistinguishable from zero. Time from pull request submission to merge rose 3.45 days on a 7.03-day baseline, a 49% increase, and the share of pull requests receiving a formal change request nearly doubled. AI review tools did not remove the bottleneck: by March 2026 nearly 80% of firms had used one, yet AI generated only 23.3% of review comments. The paper is a job-market paper with no indication of completed peer review, and it studied software work, not legal work.
